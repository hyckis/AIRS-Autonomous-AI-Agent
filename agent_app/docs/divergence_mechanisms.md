# Divergence Mechanisms
This is the document for Stage 5 of the system architecture future goal: Cognitive Diversity Engine, to prevent the research process from stopping at dominant assumptions, repeated claims, or highly convergent ideas. 

## Core Diversity Lenses

### 1. Contrarian Lens
Challenges dominant assumptions in the field and asks what research directions become possible if the opposite assumption is taken seriously.

### 2. Historical Analogy Lens
Uses historical cases, failed technologies, previous scientific debates, or earlier adoption cycles to identify alternative patterns that current research may overlook.

### 3. Cross-Disciplinary Lens
Transfers concepts from other fields to generate research directions that would not normally appear within the dominant literature of the target domain.

### 4. Failure Mode Lens
Explores what could go wrong if the dominant research direction succeeds, scales, or becomes institutionalized.

### 5. Cultural Lens
Examines how research assumptions may change across cultural, educational, linguistic, institutional, or social contexts.

### 6. Economic and Institutional Lens
Analyzes incentives, resource constraints, organizational pressures, market forces, and institutional structures that may shape or limit the research space.

### 7. Underrepresented Stakeholder Lens
Identifies users, communities, or stakeholders whose needs are not well represented in mainstream research discussions.

## Role in the System
These mechanisms are used after the Consensus & Assumption Analysis stage. The system first identifies what the field appears to agree on, then uses divergence lenses to generate alternative branches that challenge, extend, or reframe the consensus. Human researchers then review these branches in the Human Steering Layer and decide which directions should be explored further.
