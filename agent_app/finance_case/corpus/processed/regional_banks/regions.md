## 2. Investment Securities

Regions classifies debt securities as held-to-maturity when it has the
intent and ability to hold the securities to maturity. Held-to-maturity
securities are presented at amortized cost.

Debt securities not classified as held-to-maturity are classified as
available-for-sale. Available-for-sale securities are reported at
estimated fair value, with unrealized gains and losses generally
reported through accumulated other comprehensive income.

### Held-to-Maturity Securities

As of December 31, 2022:

- Amortized cost: $812 million
- Unrealized losses recognized in OCI associated with prior transfers:
  approximately $11 million
- Carrying value: approximately $801 million
- Additional unrealized losses not recognized in OCI:
  approximately $50 million
- Estimated fair value: approximately $751 million

The held-to-maturity portfolio consisted of agency
mortgage-backed securities.

### Available-for-Sale Securities

As of December 31, 2022:

- Amortized cost: $31,367 million
- Gross unrealized gains: approximately $1 million
- Gross unrealized losses: approximately $3,435 million
- Estimated fair value: $27,933 million

Major components included:

- U.S. Treasury securities
- Federal agency securities
- Residential agency mortgage-backed securities
- Commercial agency mortgage-backed securities
- Corporate and other debt securities

The largest component was residential agency mortgage-backed
securities.

Changes in interest rates materially affected the market value of the
available-for-sale securities portfolio during 2022.

Regions' accumulated other comprehensive income declined during 2022,
primarily because of unrealized losses on available-for-sale
securities and derivative instruments resulting from significant
changes in market interest rates.

Approximately $8.8 billion of debt securities were pledged at
December 31, 2022 to secure public funds, trust deposits, and other
borrowing arrangements.

## 5. Interest Rate Risk

Regions manages interest rate risk through asset-liability management,
securities, and derivative instruments.

The primary objective of its asset-liability management process is to
coordinate balance-sheet composition and interest-rate exposure so
that net interest income remains reasonably stable across different
interest-rate environments.

Regions evaluates:

- instantaneous parallel changes in interest rates
- gradual interest-rate changes
- yield-curve steepening
- yield-curve flattening
- deposit pricing and deposit mix
- prepayment behavior
- balance-sheet composition
- hedging activities

At December 31, 2022, Regions characterized its balance sheet as
asset-sensitive under both gradual and instantaneous parallel
yield-curve movements over the following twelve months.

This means that, under the modeled assumptions, increases in interest
rates were expected to increase net interest income, while decreases
in interest rates were expected to reduce net interest income.

### Estimated Net Interest Income Sensitivity

The estimates represent the change in net interest income over the
following twelve months relative to a market-forward-rate baseline.

#### Gradual Changes in Interest Rates

| Interest-rate scenario | Estimated annual change in NII |
| --- | ---: |
| +200 basis points | +$184 million |
| +100 basis points | +$101 million |
| -100 basis points | -$147 million |
| -200 basis points | -$306 million |

#### Instantaneous Changes in Interest Rates

| Interest-rate scenario | Estimated annual change in NII |
| --- | ---: |
| +200 basis points | +$201 million |
| +100 basis points | +$121 million |
| -100 basis points | -$222 million |
| -200 basis points | -$474 million |

Regions noted that deposit pricing, deposit mix, and overall
balance-sheet composition are particularly important assumptions in
these sensitivity estimates.

Its baseline forecast at year-end 2022 assumed an additional
$3 billion to $5 billion of deposit runoff during the first half of
2023 before balances stabilized and began modestly expanding.

Regions estimated that an additional $1 billion of deposit outflows
would reduce twelve-month net interest income by approximately
$26 million under the +100 basis point parallel-rate scenario.

## 6. Interest-Rate Hedging

Regions uses derivatives and debt securities to manage its interest
rate exposure.

Derivative instruments used include:

- interest-rate swaps
- interest-rate options
- interest-rate caps and floors
- forward-rate contracts
- futures
- forward-sale commitments

During the fourth quarter of 2022, Regions added approximately
$4 billion of forward-starting receive-fixed cash-flow swaps.

Approximately:

- $3 billion consisted of three-year receive-fixed swaps beginning in
  2025, with a weighted-average fixed rate of approximately 3.35%
- $1 billion consisted of six-month receive-fixed swaps beginning in
  January 2023, with a weighted-average fixed rate of approximately
  4.41%

These hedges were intended to provide additional protection against
declining interest rates and changes in future net interest income.

## 7. Relevant Risk Disclosures

Regions identified several mechanisms through which changing
interest rates could affect the bank:

- changes in the yield on loans and securities
- changes in deposit and wholesale funding costs
- changes in fixed-rate asset values
- changes in unrealized gains and losses on securities
- changes in borrower debt-service capacity
- deposit migration toward higher-yielding products
- deposit outflows
- changes in mortgage-related asset prepayments

Regions noted that increasing rates could support net interest income,
but could also increase borrower debt-service requirements and lead
to higher delinquencies or credit losses.

Persistently high interest rates and reductions in Federal Reserve
balance-sheet liquidity could also affect market liquidity and the
bank's financial condition.
