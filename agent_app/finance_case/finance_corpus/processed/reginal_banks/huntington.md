## 2. Investment Securities

Huntington classifies debt securities that it has the positive intent
and ability to hold to maturity as held-to-maturity (HTM) securities.
HTM securities are carried at amortized cost.

Other debt securities are generally classified as available-for-sale
(AFS). AFS securities are measured at fair value, with changes in fair
value generally recognized in other comprehensive income.

### Available-for-Sale Securities

As of December 31, 2022:

- Amortized cost: $27,103 million
- Fair value: $23,423 million
- Difference between amortized cost and fair value: approximately
  $3,680 million

Most of the AFS portfolio had contractual maturities greater than
10 years.

### Held-to-Maturity Securities

As of December 31, 2022:

- Amortized cost: $17,052 million
- Unrealized gains: $4 million
- Unrealized losses: $2,302 million
- Fair value: $14,754 million
- Net difference between amortized cost and fair value:
  approximately $2,298 million

The HTM portfolio consisted primarily of agency mortgage-related
securities, including residential collateralized mortgage obligations,
residential mortgage-backed securities, and commercial
mortgage-backed securities.

During 2022, Huntington transferred approximately $4.2 billion of
securities from the AFS portfolio to the HTM portfolio. At the time of
the transfer, accumulated other comprehensive income included
approximately $58 million of net unrealized losses associated with
those securities.

At December 31, 2022, approximately $26.9 billion of investment
securities were pledged to secure public and trust deposits, trading
account liabilities, repurchase agreements, U.S. Treasury demand
notes, and borrowing capacity.

## 4. Interest Rate Risk

Huntington manages interest rate risk using two primary modeling
approaches:

1. Net Interest Income at Risk (NII at Risk)
2. Economic Value of Equity at Risk (EVE at Risk)

### Net Interest Income at Risk

The NII analysis models gradual parallel shifts in interest rates over
the following twelve months.

Estimated NII sensitivity as of December 31, 2022:

| Interest-rate scenario | Estimated change in NII |
| --- | ---: |
| -100 basis points | -2.0% |
| +100 basis points | +2.0% |
| +200 basis points | +4.0% |

Management characterized the balance sheet as asset-sensitive at
December 31, 2022.

Changes in NII sensitivity were driven primarily by changes in
forecast market-interest-rate expectations and the composition of the
balance sheet.

### Economic Value of Equity at Risk

The EVE analysis models immediate parallel interest-rate shocks.

Estimated EVE sensitivity as of December 31, 2022:

| Interest-rate scenario | Estimated change in EVE |
| --- | ---: |
| -100 basis points | +5.9% |
| +100 basis points | -8.0% |
| +200 basis points | -17.3% |

Management attributed changes in EVE sensitivity during 2022 primarily
to:

- increases in the yield curve that shortened the modeled duration of
  liabilities
- changes in deposit mix
- interest-rate hedging activity

## 5. Interest-Rate and Liquidity Considerations

Changes in interest rates can affect both Huntington's earnings and
the market value of its financial assets and liabilities.

In a rising-rate environment, interest-bearing liabilities may reprice
more rapidly than interest-earning assets, potentially reducing net
interest income.

Rising interest rates also reduce the market value of fixed-rate
securities. Unrealized changes in the value of AFS securities affect
other comprehensive income and shareholders' equity, while realized
losses on securities can affect earnings and regulatory capital.

Mortgage-backed securities are particularly sensitive to interest-rate
changes because changing rates affect both their market values and
expected prepayment behavior.

Huntington's primary liquidity source is its deposit base. The
availability and cost of deposits can be affected by:

- changes in market interest rates
- rates offered by competitors
- the financial condition of Huntington or the banking industry
- changes in depositor perceptions of safety or economic value

Huntington also maintains access to other liquidity sources, including
FHLB advances, wholesale funding, securitizations, and a portfolio of
marketable securities.
