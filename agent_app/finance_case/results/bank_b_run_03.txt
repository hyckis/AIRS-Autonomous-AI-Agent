### Scenario D1: Regional Bank Contagion & Deposit Flight - Focus on Local Business Interdependence

**Dominant Assumption Challenged:** The dominant assumption is that uninsured depositors behave rationally and independently, assessing risk based solely on the bank's overall financial health. This scenario challenges that by incorporating local business interdependence – businesses rely on each other for services, credit lines, payment flows, and trust. A failure of one triggers a chain reaction among related businesses that are also customers of Bank B.

**Trigger:** A localized economic downturn significantly impacts several key industries served by Bank B (e.g., manufacturing, tourism). This could stem from a natural disaster or supply-chain disruption concentrated in the bank's footprint. The downturn leads to loan defaults within those sectors.

**Transmission Channel:** Loan defaults trigger negative media coverage and erode local confidence in Bank B. Local businesses, reliant on each other, experience cascading payment delays and credit line contractions. This sparks an exodus of uninsured deposits as businesses prioritize survival over banking relationships.  The contagion spreads beyond directly affected businesses to others within the ecosystem who perceive increased risk.

**Supporting Evidence:** [fdic_qbp_2022_q4_overview_000], [huntington_2022_10k_000], [regions_2022_10k_000] - These documents highlight the importance of community relationships for regional banks and the potential impact of adverse conditions on deposit stability.

**Distinctiveness:** This scenario differs from prior analyses by emphasizing the network effect within a local economy, rather than solely focusing on broader market sentiment or macroeconomic triggers. It prioritizes systemic risk arising from complex interdependencies rather than from asset-value declines or interest rate risk alone. 

**Quantitative Magnitude:** Requires quantitative calibration.



### Scenario D2: Commercial Real Estate Valuation Shock & Debt Spiral - Focus on Leverage and Regulatory Response

**Dominant Assumption Challenged:** The dominant assumption is that any CRE impairment will be gradual, allowing for managed adjustments to capital ratios. This scenario challenges this by incorporating a regulatory response designed to swiftly force recognition of losses. It posits that the regulator mandates immediate mark-to-market of some commercial real estate loans causing an abrupt and substantial decline in Bank B's equity.

**Trigger:** A significant increase in remote work adoption, amplified by shifts in business preferences post pandemic, leads to a rapid decline in office property values concentrated in specific geographic areas where Bank B has significant exposure. Regulators issue guidance mandating banks immediately mark-to-market a portion of their CRE loans for risk assessment purposes.

**Transmission Channel:** The regulatory mandate causes an immediate and substantial write-down on Bank B's commercial real estate loan portfolio, dramatically reducing equity capital. This triggers concerns about the bank’s solvency among investors and counterparties. While it does not cause direct defaults in other assets held by the bank, it provokes a market reassessment of CRE exposure across the sector—which may then trigger sales of securities and deposit withdrawals to meet margin calls elsewhere.

**Supporting Evidence:** [fdic_qbp_2022_q4_securities_liquidity_000] -  Highlights risks associated with unrealized losses on investment securities and potential need for liquidity. The combination of elevated unrealized losses, longer-term asset maturities, and declining deposits creates a risk that unrealized losses could become realized if banks need to sell securities to meet liquidity needs.

**Distinctiveness:** This scenario differs from prior analyses by introducing an element of regulatory action triggering immediate loss recognition, which is not explicitly included in previous scenarios. It shifts the focus away from purely market-driven declines and towards the role of regulatory intervention in amplifying losses and creating a rapid debt spiral.

**Quantitative Magnitude:** Requires quantitative calibration.
