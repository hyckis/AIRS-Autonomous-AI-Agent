*   **Transmission Channel:** The HTM portfolio ($91.327 billion, amortized cost), while not marked-to-market, suffers significant unrealized losses. ## Scenario 1: Rapid Deposit Flight due to Uninsured Deposits Concentration

**Dominant assumption or mechanism challenged:** The dominant framing often assumes deposit flight is gradual and predictable based on macroeconomic conditions. This scenario challenges the assumption that uninsured depositors behave rationally and predictably duringperiods of perceived financial stress, particularly when concentrated in specific sectors.

**Trigger:** A localized news report highlighting concerns about Bank A’s exposure to volatile technology, life science & healthcare,and private equity/venture capital sectors, coupled with a rise in regional interest rates.

**Transmission channel:** The news report triggers immediate concern among uninsured depositors heavily invested in these sectors.  They initiate rapid withdrawals due to fear of contagion or bank failure, amplified by social media and digital banking convenience. This accelerates Bank A's liquidity stress, forcing the sale of held-to-maturity securities to meet withdrawal demands.  Realized losseson these sales further erode capital and fuel negative sentiment, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop.

*   TARGET BANK PROFILE - States that 87.5% of deposits are uninsured, making the bank highly susceptible to rapid flight during periods of stress.

**Why this scenario is distinct from prior analyses:** While previous scenarios acknowledge deposit outflows, they primarily focus onbroader macroeconomic drivers. This scenario emphasizes the role of concentrated sectoral exposures and localized negative news in triggering a disproportionately swift and severe deposit run driven by uninsured depositors. The speed and potential for virality enabled by digital banking and social media are also differentiating factors.
